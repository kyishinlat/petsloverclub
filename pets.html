1. Ferrets

  • Ferrets belong to the Mustelidae family (related to otters and badgers) and are strict obligate carnivores. They are playful, high-energy, and curious.
  • Lifespan: 5 to 7 years (sometimes up to 10–12 years with great care).
  • Social Needs: Best kept in pairs or groups; they require 4 to 6 hours of supervised, out-of-cage playtime daily in a ferret-proofed space.
  • Care & Environment: They sleep 14 to 18 hours a day, have a natural musky scent, and can be prone to health issues like adrenal disease and insulinoma that lead to higher vet costs.
  • Legality: Restricted or prohibited in certain areas (such as California, Hawaii, and New York City).

2. Fancy Rats

  • Family & Nature: Intelligent, clean, and affectionate rodents that love to groom themselves and can learn tricks.
  • Lifespan: 2 to 3 years.
  • Social Needs: Extremely social animals that must be kept in same-sex pairs or groups to prevent loneliness and depression. They need about an hour of out-of-cage exercise daily.
  • Require a wire cage with good ventilation, varied enrichment (tunnels, climbing toys), and a balanced diet with regular veterinary checkups.
